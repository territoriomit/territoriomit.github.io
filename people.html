Bios

Laura Dallabrida

head of partnerships · brazil

My work is aimed at advancing spatial and environmental justice, at the intersection of water, sanitation, and climate-resilient infrastructure, with a focus on marginalized urban settlements and traditional communities. I hold a degree in Architecture and Urbanism from Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais (UFMG), where my research centered on the intrinsic relationship and conflicts between environmentally protected areas and traditional Brazilian communities (indígenas, quilombolas, ribeirinhos, caiçaras); traditional spatial knowledge and situated practices; and the planning of self-managed infrastructure, itinerant urban services and waterways for quilombola riverside communities in Vale do Ribeira, SP.

After graduating, I worked in urban planning for the municipalities of Belo Horizonte and São Paulo, most recently coordinating five integrated urban upgrading projects for favelas in watershed protection areas for the City of São Paulo's Mananciais Program. As a project coordinator, I was also the municipal representative for bilateral and multilateral partnerships working in the territories, including projects with the City of Copenhagen, C40 and UN-Habitat.

At MIT, I am interested in exploring community participation and self-organized spatial production, governance models, international financing and local delivery of infrastructure systems for climate-exposed and underserved communities in Latin America — working across scales to enable efficient, contextualized and equitable implementation.

Pietro Chiri

head of communication · peru

First-year Master in City Planning candidate at MIT's Department of Urban Studies and Planning (DUSP). I hold a degree in Architecture and Urbanism from the Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú (PUCP) and bring over four years of professional experience in public infrastructure across Peru.

Most recently, I served as architect on the Bicentennial Schools program, a landmark public–private partnership initiative launched by Peru's Ministry of Education to modernize educational infrastructure, where my work spanned both design development and technical assurance. I also contributed to Peru's post-disaster Reconstruction Program (ARCC) through ARUP, supporting healthcare and education projects across underserved regions of the country.

I am passionate about integrating innovative design into the social and economic fabric of communities, with a strong focus on urban planning, neighborhood upgrading and sustainable risk management. At MIT, my work is driven by a deep commitment to advancing equity and improving living conditions in underserved communities, particularly those in informal settlements. This focus is reflected in my participation in the DesignX Accelerator, where I have co-developed Visible, a territorial intelligence platform designed to bring data-driven planning tools to informal settlements across Latin America. I am also affiliated with GreenBridge Lab, an MIT venture focused on sustainable infrastructure decisions.

Barbara Sula

head of governance · brazil

With a background in architecture and urban planning from USP and five years of professional experience in both the private and public sectors, I've experienced how difficult it can be to move a mobility project from a vision to reality. Serving as Director of Planning in Santo André for an IDB-funded program taught me that while innovative solutions are important, they only work if the public policy and financing instruments are there to support them.

In that role I led the implementation of strategic road safety plans, coordinated gender initiatives within the transport sector, and oversaw major public infrastructure works, focusing on enhancing urban connectivity and safety through data-driven planning and international cooperation.

My goal is to focus my studies on public policy development and financing mechanisms in Latin America, and more specifically in Brazil — linking sustainable mobility solutions with public policies that actually work on the ground, and working on how to make innovation viable within a city's structures and resources. I am currently developing DYMO, a transit initiative aimed at providing shared electric mobility solutions for São Paulo, with support from the MIT Sandbox Innovation Fund Program. As a medium-term career goal, I aim to join a development bank, where I can help create the policy and financial structures that make impactful, equitable mobility systems possible.

Julieta Mulki

head of finance · argentina

Originally from Tucumán, Argentina, I hold a bachelor's degree in sociology from the University of Buenos Aires, a Master in Public Policy from Torcuato Di Tella University, and I am currently pursuing a Master in City Planning at MIT as a Fulbright scholar.

My work has centered on local governments and territorial development across Argentina. I began as Coordinator of Productive Development & Innovation at the Municipality of Tafí Viejo, where I designed employment programs and led inter-institutional coordination with UNICEF and national ministries. I then worked as a facilitator for the Local Innovation Network (RIL), strengthening the capacities of several municipal governments to drive knowledge economy policies. Most recently, I spent three years as a consultant at UNDP Argentina's Accelerator Lab, identifying and scaling grassroots innovations addressing local development challenges — from renewable energy and disability inclusion to community resilience.

At MIT, I am interested in how cities in Latin America can build more effective institutional capacities for local economic development, bridging bottom-up community innovation with the policy and financing structures needed to make it sustainable and scalable. I am also participating in MITdesignX, developing Visible, a territorial intelligence platform designed to bring data-driven planning tools to informal settlements in Argentina and Peru.
